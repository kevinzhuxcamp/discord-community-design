1 Positioning

X-Camp's Discord server is the official X-Camp Learning Hub — a live, connected community where students learn, collaborate, and grow together. Discord is chosen not by default, but deliberately: it is the most capable, widely adopted, and community-native platform available for what X-Camp needs most — real-time chat, voice collaboration, and connection with the broader coding world.

Why Discord

X-Camp's community needs go beyond what a forum or LMS can offer. Students need to ask questions and get answers in real time, not hours later. They need voice rooms to work through problems together, screen share with a TA during office hours, and feel like they belong to something alive and active — not a static message board. Discord delivers all of this natively, for free, at any scale.

Beyond X-Camp's walls, Discord is the platform where the competitive programming and coding community lives. USACO discussion groups, coding clubs, contest prep communities, and student-run coding organizations all operate on Discord. By building X-Camp's community there, students gain a natural bridge to this broader ecosystem — finding contest partners, joining coding clubs, and connecting with peers from other programs in the same space they already use for X-Camp. This is a strategic advantage no other platform can replicate.

On parent perception

Some parents associate Discord with gaming. This is a perception issue, not a platform issue — and it is entirely within X-Camp's control to address. Discord is a communication tool used by millions of students, developers, researchers, and professional communities worldwide. The X-Camp server is a private, invite-only, fully moderated environment. Parents who see the server — its named class channels, visible TA presence, structured categories, and clear rules — understand immediately that this is an academic space. X-Camp's responsibility is to show parents what the server actually looks like through orientation materials, a parent walkthrough, and clear communication. The platform does not define the community; X-Camp does.

Relationship with Discourse

The two platforms are complementary, not competing — each owns a distinct purpose:

DiscourseDiscord
Homework Q&A — forum-style, searchable, permanent knowledge baseLive chat and instant messaging
Structured solved-answer threads that compound in value over timeVoice calls, screen sharing, and office hours
Student bonding, community sharing, and track lounges
Public-facing community open to outside coding enthusiasts

3 Student tracks — community identity

Inspired by the house system in Harry Potter, X-Camp students choose a track when they first join the server. Tracks are not academic levels — they reflect a student's coding identity, goals, and personality. A student in CS300 and a student in CS500 can both be Competitors, and that shared identity becomes the basis for friendship, rivalry, and community beyond the classroom.

Each student carries two roles on their Discord profile: their class role (e.g. Student-CS300) and their track role (e.g. 🏆 Competitor). Tracks are self-selected, low-stakes, and can be changed — the goal is to give students a sense of belonging and a community of like-minded peers, not to box them in.

🏆 The Competitor
Laser-focused on USACO, rankings, and algorithmic mastery. Motivated by challenge, speed, and recognition. Wants to solve hard problems and climb leaderboards.
Motto: "Every problem has a solution. Find it faster."
🔬 The Builder
Pursuing CS, AI, or STEM seriously. Interested in how code connects to research, real-world applications, and a future career in technology or academia.
Motto: "Code is a tool. Build something that matters."
🎨 The Explorer
Treats coding as a creative hobby or fun extracurricular. No pressure, just curiosity. Wants to learn, build interesting things, and enjoy the process for its own sake.
Motto: "Code because it's fun. That's reason enough."
🚀 The Founder
Entrepreneurial mindset. Wants to build projects, apps, or maybe a startup someday. Interested in turning ideas into real products that other people actually use.
Motto: "Don't just learn to code. Build something."

When a student completes verification and enters the server, the bot immediately prompts them: "Welcome to X-Camp! One more step — choose your track." Four buttons appear. The student taps once and is assigned their track role. They can change it anytime by returning to #choose-your-track.

Design principles

Tracks are identity, not gatekeeping
A student can change their track anytime. There are no wrong answers. The goal is to help students find their people — not to lock them into a category.
Tracks cut across class levels
A CS100 Explorer and a CS500 Explorer share the same lounge — creating mentorship moments and cross-level friendships. Senior students naturally become informal role models.
Staff engagement per track
Assign one TA or instructor as a track champion for each group — someone who checks in, seeds discussions, and runs the track-specific AMA. This prevents lounges from going quiet.
Use track data wisely
Track distribution tells X-Camp something useful about its student body — contest-driven vs. career-driven vs. hobbyist — informing curriculum design, marketing, and event planning.

5 Student identity & nicknames

Without a clear naming standard, teachers and TAs have no way to know who they're talking to in class channels. A student named "xXdark_coderXx" is unidentifiable. This section defines how X-Camp ensures every student is recognizable by name across the server.

Recommended approach: enforced server nickname

During the verification step, the bot automatically sets each student's server nickname to a standard format using their real first name and X-Camp ID:

Nickname format

FirstName [XC-12345]  — e.g. Sarah [XC-84921]

First name only for a friendlier feel. The X-Camp ID in brackets provides traceability for staff. The student's global Discord username is unchanged — only their display name within the X-Camp server is formatted this way.

If a student tries to change their nickname back, the bot detects the change and resets it automatically. Full name and ID mappings are stored privately in #enrollment-ops for coordinator reference.

How it works technically

When the student runs !verify [X-Camp ID], the bot calls Discord's PATCH /guilds/{guild.id}/members/{user.id} API to set their server nickname in the same step as role assignment. No separate action is needed by coordinators. The bot also listens for the GUILD_MEMBER_UPDATE event — if a student changes their nickname, the bot resets it within seconds.

6 Student role management

Student roles need to be granted at enrollment and revoked at quarter end — across potentially hundreds of students and 17 course levels. Getting this right is critical: students who keep roles after their quarter ends can still access class channels they are no longer enrolled in, and manual management at scale is error-prone.

Core challenge

X-Camp runs multiple classes per level, with different teachers and class times. A student enrolled in CS300 should only see the CS300 category. At quarter end, their access must be cleanly revoked without coordinators manually updating hundreds of roles.

Option comparison

Option A — Manual by coordinator
Coordinator manually assigns and removes roles at the start and end of each quarter based on the enrollment list.
✓ No technical setup required
✓ Full coordinator control
✗ Time-consuming at scale
✗ Prone to human error
✗ Doesn't scale as enrollment grows
Option B — X-Camp ID verification
Each student's unique X-Camp ID serves as their verification credential. Bot validates ID, assigns role, sets nickname, and marks the ID as used. Quarter-end bulk removal via a single admin command.
✓ Students onboard themselves
✓ No coordinator bottleneck
✓ Bulk removal is one command
✓ Scales to any enrollment size
✗ Requires enrollment system integration
⭐ Option C — API sync Recommended long-term
A script reads the enrollment database and calls Discord's API to assign and remove roles automatically on a schedule or triggered by enrollment changes.
✓ Fully automated — zero manual work
✓ Roles update in real time
✓ Quarter-end removal is automatic
✓ Single source of truth
✗ Requires one-time developer setup
Option D — Reaction roles (simplest)
Students react with an emoji to self-assign their level role. Quarter-end bulk removal via Carl-bot's mass-role remove command.
✓ Easiest to set up — no coding
✓ Students self-serve
✗ No validation — students can assign any level
✗ Requires coordinator spot-check
Recommended now: Option B · Long-term: Option C

Option B (X-Camp ID verification) is the right starting point — no custom development needed, ties directly to enrollment, and handles nickname assignment in the same step. Option C (API sync) becomes the right investment in Year 2 as enrollment scales.

7 Homework night workflow

Runs daily during the quarter, 5–8 PM PT. A bot-assisted queue ensures fair TA assignment and builds a searchable resolved-answers archive over time.

1
Bot opens HW Night
At 4:55 PM, bot auto-posts in #hw-night-lobby: "HW Night starts in 5 minutes — TAs are online!" Voice rooms unlock.
automated
2
Student posts question
Student uses !question or posts in #ask-a-question with level tag e.g. [CS200]. Bot timestamps, queues, and adds a ticket emoji.
student
3
TA claims question
TA reacts with hand emoji or uses !claim. Bot notifies student. If voice is needed, student is moved to an available HW Night Room.
TA action
4
Question resolved
TA uses !solved. Bot archives thread to #solved-answers. Student rates helpfulness 1–5 stars.
TA closes
5
Bot closes HW Night
At 8:00 PM, bot posts nightly summary: questions answered, avg response time, students helped. Voice rooms lock. Unanswered questions flagged for review.
auto-summary
📋 Queue rules
One active question per student at a time. Questions must include the problem name and what the student already tried. Duplicate questions are linked to existing resolved threads.
👥 TA coverage model
Each level group (100s, 200s, 300s, 400s, 500s+) has at least one TA on duty per HW Night. Coordinator monitors queue depth and can assign additional TAs if wait time exceeds 10 minutes.

8 Office hours workflow

Scheduled 1-on-1 TA support sessions using Discord's Forum channel + Ticket Tool bot. Ticket Tool creates a private thread between the student and TA — keeping session details confidential while still logging the interaction for coordinator oversight.

1
Student opens a ticket
Student clicks the "Book Office Hours" button pinned in their class channel. Ticket Tool prompts them to describe their question. A private thread is created visible only to the student, TAs for that level, and coordinators.
student
2
TA claims the session
An available TA claims the ticket and agrees on a voice time within the private thread using Sesh bot to show available slots.
TA action
3
Bot sends reminder
10 minutes before the agreed time, bot DMs both student and TA. Student is reminded to have their question and code ready.
automated
4
Session in voice
Student joins the Office Hours voice room. TA screen shares, walks through code, and uses the whiteboard. Default session cap is 20 minutes.
TA + student
5
TA closes ticket with summary
TA posts a brief session summary and closes with !close. With student's permission, key insight can be posted to #solved-answers to benefit classmates.
TA closesoptional public post
📋 Session rules
One active ticket per student at a time. Sessions capped at 20 minutes. Students must describe their question when opening the ticket — vague requests are asked to clarify before a TA claims.
👥 TA availability
TAs post weekly available slots in #ta-schedule. Coordinator ensures each level band has at least 3 office hours slots per week. Demand is tracked via Ticket Tool analytics.

The thread that connects it all

A student who joins X-Camp as a nervous CS100 Seedling and leaves as a Legend alumni has spent years in this community. Their XP, badges, track identity, friendships, and memories all live here. The Discord server is not just a support tool — it is the X-Camp experience itself.

Teachers own their class category on Discord. Their weekly presence is structured around two required check-in days and a weekend class, with Discord serving as the bridge between sessions — not a replacement for the learning site.

13 Cost

Running a Discord community at X-Camp's scale is significantly more affordable than any comparable platform. Discord itself is free regardless of server size — all core features including voice, chat, threads, roles, and moderation tools are available at no cost.

Bot costs

MEE6 or AtlasBasic XP, leveling, welcome messages~$4–11/moStart free; upgrade if advanced XP customization is needed

Cost summary

ScenarioMonthly costNotes

Standard operations~$15–30/moMEE6 and Ticket Tool upgraded for better XP and queue analytics

For comparison

Slack at X-Camp's student scale would cost $2,000+/month. Circle starts at $89/month. Discord's platform cost is effectively zero — the only real cost is bot tooling at $0–50/month, and staff time.

The most significant cost of running this community is staff time — coordinator daily monitoring, teacher Tue/Thu check-ins, and TA coverage for HW Night every evening. These are operational commitments, not platform fees.

Build server structure, all categories, channels, and roles · Configure permission matrix · Install and configure all bots · Set up X-Camp ID verification and nickname enforcement · Write rules and welcome message · Internal staff dry run
Week 3
Staff onboarding
Onboard all teachers, TAs, and coordinators · Assign roles and class category ownership · Run mock HW Night and office hours with staff only · Test X-Camp ID verification and nickname flow end-to-end · Finalize moderation and escalation procedures

Invite one pilot class (recommended: CS300) · Send parent communication letter · Test verification flow with real students · Gather feedback on channel structure, HW Night, and office hours · Iterate before full rollout
Weeks 5–6
Full rollout

Month 2+

Activate alumni channel · Run first quarter-end role removal cycle and refine process · Begin planning API sync (Option C) for automated role management · Consider public server discovery for CS500+ · Monthly server health review via Statbot
