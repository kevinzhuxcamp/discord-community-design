1 Positioning

X-Camp's Discord server is the official X-Camp Learning Hub — a live, connected community where students learn, collaborate, and grow together. Discord is chosen not by default, but deliberately: it is the most capable, widely adopted, and community-native platform available for what X-Camp needs most — real-time chat, voice collaboration, and connection with the broader coding world.

Why Discord

X-Camp's community needs go beyond what a forum or LMS can offer. Students need to ask questions and get answers in real time, not hours later. They need voice rooms to work through problems together, screen share with a TA during office hours, and feel like they belong to something alive and active — not a static message board. Discord delivers all of this natively, for free, at any scale.

Beyond X-Camp's walls, Discord is the platform where the competitive programming and coding community lives. USACO discussion groups, coding clubs, contest prep communities, and student-run coding organizations all operate on Discord. By building X-Camp's community there, students gain a natural bridge to this broader ecosystem — finding contest partners, joining coding clubs, and connecting with peers from other programs in the same space they already use for X-Camp. This is a strategic advantage no other platform can replicate.

On parent perception

Some parents associate Discord with gaming. This is a perception issue, not a platform issue — and it is entirely within X-Camp's control to address. Discord is a communication tool used by millions of students, developers, researchers, and professional communities worldwide. The X-Camp server is a private, invite-only, fully moderated environment. Parents who see the server — its named class channels, visible TA presence, structured categories, and clear rules — understand immediately that this is an academic space. X-Camp's responsibility is to show parents what the server actually looks like through orientation materials, a parent walkthrough, and clear communication. The platform does not define the community; X-Camp does.

Relationship with Discourse

X-Camp currently uses Discourse for homework Q&A, and it does that job well. Discourse is a forum — it is designed for structured, searchable, long-form discussion where answers persist and accumulate value over time. Discord is a community platform — it is designed for real-time interaction, voice, and the kind of fluid back-and-forth that makes students feel connected.

These two tools are complementary, not competing. The recommended approach is to run both in parallel with a clear division of purpose:

NeedPlatformWhy
Live chat and instant messagingDiscordReal-time, threaded, with reactions and voice — built for conversation
Voice calls and screen sharingDiscordNative voice channels and video — no third-party tool needed
Homework Q&A knowledge baseDiscourseSearchable, permanent, solved-answer format — compounds in value over time
Student and community bondingDiscordCommunity channels, voice study halls, contests, leaderboards
Contest and coding communityDiscordWhere USACO, coding clubs, and competitive programming communities already live
Office hours / HW Night schedulingDiscordTicket Tool bot manages 1-on-1 booking and HW Night queue; Sesh bot handles TA availability calendars and session reminders — all within Discord without external scheduling tools
Alumni and outreach communityDiscordPublic server discovery, alumni voice, connection to outside coding world

Over time, as Discord's forum channels and thread features mature, some Discourse Q&A workflows may migrate to Discord entirely. For now, maintaining both platforms with clear ownership keeps disruption low and leverages what each does best.

3 Student tracks — community identity

Inspired by the house system in Harry Potter, X-Camp students choose a track when they first join the server. Tracks are not academic levels — they reflect a student's coding identity, goals, and personality. A student in CS300 and a student in CS500 can both be Competitors, and that shared identity becomes the basis for friendship, rivalry, and community beyond the classroom.

Each student carries two roles on their Discord profile: their class role (e.g. Student-CS300) and their track role (e.g. 🏆 Competitor). Tracks are self-selected, low-stakes, and can be changed — the goal is to give students a sense of belonging and a community of like-minded peers, not to box them in.

🏆 The Competitor
Laser-focused on USACO, rankings, and algorithmic mastery. Motivated by challenge, speed, and recognition. Wants to solve hard problems and climb leaderboards.
Motto: "Every problem has a solution. Find it faster."
🔬 The Builder
Pursuing CS, AI, or STEM seriously. Interested in how code connects to research, real-world applications, and a future career in technology or academia.
Motto: "Code is a tool. Build something that matters."
🎨 The Explorer
Treats coding as a creative hobby or fun extracurricular. No pressure, just curiosity. Wants to learn, build interesting things, and enjoy the process for its own sake.
Motto: "Code because it's fun. That's reason enough."
🚀 The Founder
Entrepreneurial mindset. Wants to build projects, apps, or maybe a startup someday. Interested in turning ideas into real products that other people actually use.
Motto: "Don't just learn to code. Build something."

How it works on Discord

When a student completes the enrollment code verification and enters the server, the bot immediately prompts them in a private welcome thread: "Welcome to X-Camp! One more step — choose your track. This tells us what kind of coder you are and connects you with like-minded students." Four buttons appear, one per track. The student taps once and is assigned their track role. They can change it anytime by returning to #choose-your-track.

Design principles

Tracks are identity, not gatekeeping
A student can change their track anytime. There are no wrong answers. The goal is to help students find their people — not to lock them into a category. Some students may feel they belong to two tracks; encourage them to pick the one that feels most like them right now.
Tracks cut across class levels
A CS100 Explorer and a CS500 Explorer share the same lounge. This is intentional — it creates mentorship moments, cross-level friendships, and a sense that the community is bigger than any one class. Senior students naturally become informal role models.
Staff engagement per track
Assign one TA or instructor as a track champion for each group — someone who checks in, seeds discussions, and runs the track-specific AMA. This prevents lounges from going quiet after the novelty wears off.
Use track data wisely
Track distribution data tells X-Camp something useful about its student body — how many are contest-driven vs. career-driven vs. hobbyist. This can inform curriculum design, marketing messaging, and what kinds of events to run more of.

4 Student role management

Student roles need to be granted at enrollment and revoked at quarter end — across potentially hundreds of students and 17 course levels. Getting this right is critical: students who keep roles after their quarter ends can still access class channels they are no longer enrolled in, and manual management at scale is error-prone.

Core challenge

X-Camp runs multiple classes per level, with different teachers and class times. A student enrolled in CS300 (Section A, Mon/Wed) should only see the CS300 category — not CS400, not other sections. At quarter end, their access must be cleanly revoked so the server stays accurate and secure without coordinator manually updating hundreds of roles.

Option comparison

Option A — Manual by coordinator
Coordinator manually assigns and removes roles in Discord at the start and end of each quarter based on the enrollment list.
✓ No technical setup required
✓ Full coordinator control
✗ Time-consuming at scale
✗ Prone to human error
✗ Doesn't scale as enrollment grows
Option B — Self-serve enrollment codes
Enrollment system generates a unique code per student per quarter. Student enters the code on joining Discord. Bot assigns the correct role automatically. Quarter-end bulk removal via a single admin command.
✓ Students onboard themselves
✓ No coordinator bottleneck
✓ Bulk removal is one command
✓ Scales to any enrollment size
✗ Requires enrollment system integration
✗ Codes can be shared (mitigated by one-time use)
⭐ Option C — API sync with enrollment system Recommended
A script (Google Apps Script, Zapier, or custom) reads your enrollment database and calls Discord's API to assign and remove roles automatically on a schedule or triggered by enrollment changes.
✓ Fully automated — zero manual work
✓ Roles update in real time when enrollment changes
✓ Quarter-end removal happens automatically
✓ Single source of truth (your enrollment system)
✗ Requires developer setup (one-time)
✗ Needs Discord API token and enrollment system access
Option D — Carl-bot reaction roles (simplest)
Coordinator posts a message listing all class levels. Students react with the corresponding emoji to self-assign. Quarter-end bulk removal via Carl-bot's mass-role remove command.
✓ Easiest to set up — no coding
✓ Students self-serve
✗ Students can assign any level (no validation)
✗ Requires coordinator spot-check against enrollment list
✗ Quarter-end removal is still a manual command

Recommended approach: enrollment code verification (Option B)

For X-Camp's current scale, Option B strikes the best balance between automation and simplicity. It requires no custom development — just a bot (Carl-bot or MEE6) configured to validate codes — and it ties role assignment directly to your enrollment process.

Long-term goal

As X-Camp grows, Option C (API sync) becomes the right investment. A one-time developer setup means roles are always in sync with your enrollment database with zero ongoing manual work. This is worth prioritizing in Year 2.

Runs daily during the quarter, 5–8 PM PT. A bot-assisted queue ensures fair TA assignment and builds a searchable resolved-answers archive over time.

📋 Queue rules
One active question per student at a time. Questions must include the problem name and what the student already tried. Duplicate questions are linked to existing resolved threads.
👥 TA coverage model
Each level group (100s, 200s, 300s, 400s, 500s+) has at least one TA on duty per HW Night. Coordinator monitors queue depth and can assign additional TAs if wait time exceeds 10 minutes.

6 Office hours workflow

Scheduled 1-on-1 TA support sessions. Students book a private session with a TA from their level band. Office hours use Discord's Forum channel + Ticket Tool bot in combination: the Forum channel provides a structured, searchable record of each session request, while Ticket Tool manages the private thread and TA assignment.

Why Forum + Ticket Tool, not just one or the other

A plain Forum channel is visible to all students in the class, which suits open Q&A but not private 1-on-1 sessions. Ticket Tool creates a private thread between the student and their assigned TA — keeping session details confidential — while still logging the interaction for coordinator oversight. The combination gives you privacy, structure, and searchability.

1
Student opens a ticket
Student clicks the "Book Office Hours" button pinned in their class channel. Ticket Tool prompts them to select their level and briefly describe their question. A private thread is created visible only to the student, TAs for that level, and coordinators.
student
2
TA claims the session
An available TA for that level claims the ticket. Bot notifies the student and both parties agree on a voice time within the private thread. TA posts their available slots or uses Sesh bot to show a bookable calendar.
TA action
3
Bot sends reminder
10 minutes before the agreed time, bot DMs both student and TA. Student is reminded to have their question and code ready.
automated
4
Session in voice
Student joins the Office Hours voice room. TA can screen share, walk through code, and use the whiteboard. Default session cap is 20 minutes. TA can extend if needed.
TA + student
5
TA closes ticket with summary
TA types a brief session summary in the private thread and closes the ticket with !close. Summary is logged for coordinator review. With the student's permission, key insight can be posted to #solved-answers to benefit classmates.
TA closesoptional public post
📋 Session rules
One active office hours ticket per student at a time. Sessions capped at 20 minutes by default. Students must describe their question when opening the ticket — vague requests will be asked to clarify before a TA claims.
👥 TA availability
TAs post their weekly available slots in #ta-schedule at the start of each week. Coordinator ensures each level band has at least 3 available office hours slots per week. Demand is tracked via Ticket Tool analytics.

8 Engagement & gamification

Gamification works best when earning rewards feels like a natural byproduct of being a good student and community member — not a separate game layered on top. Every point, badge, and title in X-Camp's system is designed to recognize behaviors that are genuinely valuable: helping classmates, showing up consistently, improving over time, and contributing to the community.

Design principle

Avoid rewarding raw activity volume (e.g. most messages sent) — this creates spam incentives. Instead reward quality, consistency, helpfulness, and growth. A student who answers three questions well should earn more than a student who sends thirty one-word messages.

XP system — how points are earned

XP is the foundation of the gamification system, tracked by MEE6 or Atlas bot. Points are awarded for meaningful actions, with diminishing returns built in to prevent farming.

Spam posting for XPMinimum message length enforced by bot; XP per message capped at 5 per hour
Fake upvoting between friendsUpvotes from accounts under 7 days old don't count; same-user upvotes on mutual posts are filtered
Claiming solved answers unfairlyOnly TAs and teachers can mark answers as solved — students cannot self-award
AFK farming in voice channelsVoice XP (if enabled) requires periodic activity checks via bot; muted + deafened = no XP
Alt accounts / duplicate joinsEach student is issued a unique X-Camp ID at enrollment. This ID is required during the Discord verification step — the bot validates the ID against the enrollment database before assigning any role. One X-Camp ID can only be used to verify one Discord account. If a student needs to change their Discord account, they must contact a coordinator to reset the ID link
Long-term vision

As the community matures, top-ranked students (Champion and Legend) can be invited to become community TAs, event hosts, or track champions — turning the most engaged students into community leaders. This creates a self-sustaining pipeline where recognition leads to responsibility, and the community grows from within.

Full student lifespan — first class to alumni

Zoom out further and the Discord community becomes a thread that runs through a student's entire X-Camp journey — from nervous beginner to confident alumnus.

Quarter 1 — The new student
Finding their footing
Student joins as a 🌱 Seedling. Everything is new — new classmates, new problems, new platform. They mostly ask questions, attend HW Nights when stuck, and lurk in their track lounge. By quarter end they've introduced themselves, hit their first streak, and maybe answered one or two questions for classmates. Rank: ⚡ Coder. They feel like they belong.
Quarters 2–3 — The regular
Building identity and relationships
Student knows the rhythm. HW Nights are routine. They recognize names, have a few Discord friends, and are starting to answer questions as often as they ask them. Their track identity feels real — a Competitor is grinding USACO problems, a Founder has shared their first project. XP climbs steadily. Rank reaches 🔥 Hacker or 💡 Innovator. They start showing up to inter-track events for fun, not just XP.
Quarters 4–6 — The contributor
Giving back to the community
Student has advanced class levels. They are now one of the more experienced voices in the community. Newer students ask them questions. They volunteer to co-host a track AMA, suggest a problem for Problem of the Week, or help a Seedling who's exactly where they were a year ago. Rank reaches 🏅 Champion. Their profile shows a collection of badges that tells the story of their X-Camp journey. They are recognized by name.
Final quarter — The veteran
Closing the chapter
Student is finishing their X-Camp journey. They're in the CS600s or have completed the curriculum. Their Discord presence carries weight — newer students look up to them. They participate in their last end-of-quarter showcase, receive their final badges, and if they've reached 7,000 XP, earn the permanent 🌟 Legend rank. At quarter end, their class role is removed — but their XP, badges, and community identity stay.
Post-graduation — The alumni
A new kind of belonging
Alumni role replaces their student role. They gain access to #alumni-lounge, #internships-jobs, and #college-advice. The broader class channels are no longer visible — but the community channels and their track lounge remain. Legends are invited into the alumni mentor program, where they occasionally drop into lower-level class channels (read-only) and host AMAs for current students. They become the living proof that X-Camp works — and the most powerful recruitment asset the community has.
The thread that connects it all

A student who joins X-Camp as a nervous CS100 Seedling and leaves as a Legend alumni has spent years in this community. Their XP, badges, track identity, friendships, and memories all live here. The Discord server is not just a support tool — it is the X-Camp experience itself, running alongside every class, every quarter, every milestone. That continuity is what transforms a coding school into a community.
